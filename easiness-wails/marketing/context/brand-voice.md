# Easiness — Brand Voice Guide

This file defines how Easiness should sound in all marketing content.
Give this to Claude or NotebookLM before generating any copy.

---

## Brand personality

Easiness is like a **trusted, smart friend who understands your business**.
Not a cold corporate software company. Not a pushy salesperson.
Someone who genuinely understands the daily frustrations of running a small business
and has a practical solution.

**Three words that describe the Easiness voice:**
1. **Clear** — Simple language, no jargon. If a 16-year-old helping at a shop can understand it, it's right.
2. **Confident** — We know the product works. We don't hedge or over-qualify.
3. **Empathetic** — We acknowledge the real struggles of running a small business before offering a solution.

## Tone by channel

| Channel | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Facebook | Friendly, conversational, a little casual | "Ever lose track of stock? You're not alone." |
| LinkedIn | Professional but human, not corporate | "Managing a distribution business means tracking hundreds of supplier invoices." |
| Email | Direct, respectful, gets to the point fast | "I'll be brief — here's what Easiness does and why it might matter to you." |
| Website | Clear, benefit-driven, structured | "Easiness tracks your stock, invoices, and profit — all in one place." |
| WhatsApp | Personal, brief, friendly | "Hi [Name], quick question — are you using any software to manage your shop's stock?" |

## Words and phrases TO USE

- "know exactly" (gives certainty)
- "instantly" (speed)
- "works offline" (reliability)
- "one place" (simplicity)
- "your data, your computer" (privacy/control)
- "no monthly fees" (clear pricing benefit)
- "in minutes" (ease of setup)
- "profit on every sale" (financial insight)
- "never lose track of stock again" (solving a real pain)
- "runs on your computer" (tangible, not abstract)

---

## Words and phrases to AVOID

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|------------|
| "synergy" | (don't use this ever) |
| "leverage" | "use" |
| "robust solution" | "software that actually works" |
| "end-to-end" | describe the actual flow |
| "seamless" | "smooth" or be specific |
| "cutting-edge technology" | irrelevant to customers |
| "cloud-based" | "accessible from your browser" |
| "intuitive interface" | "easy to use" or show a screenshot |
| "streamline your operations" | "save time" or "reduce mistakes" |
| "scalable" | only use if describing multi-location growth |

## How to structure a marketing message

Every effective marketing message follows this structure:

1. **Hook** — State the problem (the customer must recognise this as their own problem)
2. **Agitate** — Make the problem feel real (what does it cost them?)
3. **Solution** — Introduce Easiness as the answer
4. **Proof** — Show a feature, a screenshot, or a number
5. **Call to action** — Tell them exactly what to do next

**Short example:**
> You're managing your shop's stock in a notebook. [HOOK]
> Every time you run out of something, you lose a sale — or you buy too much and it sits there. [AGITATE]
> Easiness tracks every product you buy and sell, so you always know what you have. [SOLUTION]
> It updates automatically when you create a purchase order or invoice. [PROOF]
> Try it free for 14 days. [CTA]

## What Easiness is NOT

Do not position Easiness as:
- An accounting/tax software (it doesn't do tax filing or payroll)
- An enterprise ERP (it's not SAP or Oracle)
- A point-of-sale (POS) system (no barcode scanner integration at this stage)
- A mobile app (it's a desktop app; mobile browser access is optional via sync)

## Customer language (use their words, not ours)

The best marketing uses the exact words customers use to describe their problems.
These are real phrases small business owners use:

- "I don't know where the money went"
- "My stock register is always wrong"
- "I forget what I owe my suppliers"
- "I don't know if I'm making money or losing money"
- "Customers claim they paid but I have no record"
- "I have to count everything by hand at month end"
- "My Excel sheet got deleted and I lost everything"

Use these phrases in your copy. When customers read their own words, they feel understood.
