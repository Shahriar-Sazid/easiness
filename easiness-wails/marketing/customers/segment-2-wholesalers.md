# Segment 2 — Wholesale & Distribution Businesses

**Priority: MEDIUM**
**Reach via: LinkedIn, email, trade associations, referrals from Segment 1**

---

## Who this person is

A wholesale or distribution business owner buys products in bulk from manufacturers or importers,
then resells them to retailers, small shops, or directly to businesses.

**Typical businesses in this segment:**
- Food and beverage distributors
- Household goods wholesalers
- Agricultural product distributors
- Electrical parts and accessories wholesalers
- Paper and stationery distributors
- Pharmaceutical wholesalers (non-prescription)
- Building materials suppliers

**Business size:** 2–30 staff, with a dedicated sales team and warehouse staff
**Annual revenue:** $100,000 – $5,000,000
**Tech comfort:** Owner uses email and LinkedIn; may have a dedicated admin or accountant
**Current tools:** Likely using a combination of Excel, manual invoices, and possibly QuickBooks or basic accounting software

---

## Their daily reality

This person manages more complex operations than a retail shop owner:
- Multiple suppliers, each with different payment terms
- Many customers (retailers) buying on credit — some paying late or disputing invoices
- A warehouse or storage location that must be tracked carefully
- Larger purchase orders and sales orders involving many line items
- A team that needs to see the same data: the salesperson, the warehouse manager, the owner

Their biggest fear: an invoice dispute they can't prove. A customer says they paid — and the business owner has no clean record.

Their second biggest fear: a stockout when a customer places a large order.

---

## What they care about most (in order)

1. **Supplier account management** — Accurate records of what is owed to each supplier, payment history, and outstanding balances.
2. **Customer receivables** — Which customers owe money and how much. Who is overdue.
3. **Stock levels across locations** — They often have a main warehouse plus a secondary storage. They need to see both.
4. **Profit margins per product** — Buying in bulk and selling at varying prices makes it easy to lose track of actual margins.
5. **Professionalism** — Their customers are businesses, so they expect proper PDF invoices with line items and totals.
6. **Multi-user access** — The owner, the salesperson, and the admin all need to see the same data.
