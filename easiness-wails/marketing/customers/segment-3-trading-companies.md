# Segment 3 — Trading & Import/Export Companies

**Priority: MEDIUM**
**Reach via: LinkedIn, email, Chamber of Commerce, import/export associations**

---

## Who this person is

A trading or import/export company sources products from one country or region and resells them
in another, or acts as a middleman between manufacturers and retailers.

**Typical businesses in this segment:**
- General trading companies
- Import agents (bring goods in from overseas suppliers)
- Export agents (source locally and sell internationally)
- Commodity traders (agricultural produce, metals, raw materials)
- Electronics or consumer goods importers
- Textile and fabric importers/distributors

**Business size:** 2–20 staff; often a lean core team plus agents or contractors
**Annual revenue:** $200,000 – $10,000,000 (highly variable — one big shipment can be a year's revenue)
**Tech comfort:** Owner is often well-travelled, business-savvy, and comfortable with computers and email
**Current tools:** Mix of Excel, email chains, WhatsApp groups, and possibly basic accounting software

---

## Their daily reality

The trading company's world is about relationships, timing, and margin:
- They buy in large quantities, often in a single shipment, and resell over weeks or months
- They deal with multiple currencies, shipment costs, and supplier invoices in different formats
- They have to know their exact landed cost before they can price accurately
- Outstanding receivables can be very large — one unpaid invoice can damage cash flow significantly
- They store goods in warehouses they may or may not own

Their biggest operational fear: placing a large order from a supplier when they don't actually know if previous goods have sold through and been paid for.

## How they currently manage (the competition you are replacing)

| What they use | Why it fails them |
|---------------|------------------|
| Excel with per-shipment sheets | No cross-shipment view; difficult to get a total profit picture |
| QuickBooks or accounting software | Does not handle inventory per shipment; no product-level profitability |
| Email chains | Cannot search for supplier balance; disputes are hard to resolve |
| Manual per-shipment profit calculations | Error-prone; doesn't account for all costs |
| Multiple disconnected apps | Each covers one part; no integrated picture |

---

## What they care about most (in order)

1. **Supplier account accuracy** — They deal with overseas suppliers where a dispute is costly. They need clean records.
2. **Product profitability tracking** — They need to know which product lines are actually profitable after all costs.
3. **Customer receivables** — Their customers are often businesses buying on 30–60 day credit terms. Tracking who has paid and who hasn't is critical.
4. **Stock visibility** — They need to know how much inventory they have on hand, in a warehouse, or in transit.
5. **Professional invoicing** — Their customers expect formal, professional documents — not handwritten receipts.
6. **Data privacy** — They don't want sensitive trade data on a public cloud server.
