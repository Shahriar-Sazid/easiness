# Prompt: Cold Email — Segment 1 (Retail Shop Owners)

## Prompt

```
You are a marketing copywriter for a software product called Easiness.

I have provided you with context files about the product, its features and benefits, pricing, brand voice, and the target customer.

Write a cold email sequence (3 emails) targeting small retail shop owners.

Context for email sequence:
- Email 1 is the first contact — they have never heard of Easiness
- Email 2 is a follow-up if they did not reply to Email 1 (send 4 days later)
- Email 3 is the final follow-up if still no reply (send 4 days after Email 2)

Requirements for all three emails:
- Subject line must be compelling and specific — not generic ("Quick question" is better than "Software for your business")
- Keep each email under 150 words (short emails get more replies than long ones)
- The tone is friendly and direct — like a message from a real person, not a company
- Do NOT use: "I hope this email finds you well", "synergy", "robust", "leverage", "seamless", "streamline"
- Do not pitch the product in the first sentence — lead with the problem
- Include one specific benefit or proof point in each email (different in each)
- End with a single, low-friction call to action (e.g. "Would a 10-minute demo be worth your time?" not "Buy now")
- Each email should stand alone — the reader may only see one of them

Requirements for specific emails:
- Email 1: Lead with a stock control or profit question. The goal is a reply or click.
- Email 2: Acknowledge they are busy. Lead with a different pain point (e.g. credit customers who haven't paid). Slightly more specific.
- Email 3: Short and direct. Offer to close the loop — "if this isn't relevant, no worries, just say so."

Output format:
EMAIL 1
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 2
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 3
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]
```

## How to send cold emails effectively

**Who to send to:**
- Shop owners in your target area — find them in local business directories, Google Maps ("grocery stores in [city]"), or Facebook business pages
- Always use the owner's name if you have it — "Hi Sarah" outperforms "Hi there" significantly

**When to send:**
- Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday mornings — these have the highest open rates
- Avoid Mondays (too busy) and Fridays (mentally checked out)
- Send at 9am or 2pm in the recipient's local time

**Tools for sending:**
- Gmail with a mail merge tool (like Yet Another Mail Merge or Mailmeteor) — free for small lists
- Mailchimp — free for up to 500 contacts; tracks opens and clicks

**What counts as a success:**
- Open rate above 30% = good subject line
- Reply rate above 5% = good email content
- If below these, adjust the subject line first, then the body

**After a reply:**
Respond within 2 hours if possible. Offer a specific time for a call or demo: "I'm free Thursday at 3pm or Friday at 10am — would either work?"
