# Prompt: Cold Email — Segment 2 (Wholesale & Distribution)

**Before using this prompt:** Upload the four context files and `segment-2-wholesalers.md` to Claude or NotebookLM.

## Prompt

```
You are a marketing copywriter for a software product called Easiness.

I have provided you with context files about the product, its features and benefits, pricing, brand voice, and the target customer.

Write a cold email sequence (3 emails) targeting wholesale and distribution business owners.

Context for email sequence:
- Email 1 is the first contact — they have never heard of Easiness
- Email 2 is a follow-up if they did not reply to Email 1 (send 5 days later)
- Email 3 is the final follow-up if still no reply (send 5 days after Email 2)

Key differences from Segment 1 (retail) outreach:
- This audience is more professional and expects a more polished, business-to-business tone
- They have larger operations — reference the scale: multiple accounts, warehouse locations, team access
- They likely have tried other software before — acknowledge this; don't assume they are using nothing
- The pain that resonates most: receivables management (who owes them money) and stock visibility
- Lead with business impact — time lost, money at risk — not just features

Requirements for all three emails:
- Keep each email under 200 words
- Use a professional but human tone — no corporate buzzwords
- Do NOT use: "I hope this email finds you well", "synergy", "leverage", "robust", "seamless"
- Lead with a specific, relatable business problem in each email
- Do NOT pitch features in the first email — pitch the outcome
- Include a low-friction CTA: "Would a 15-minute call be worth your time?"
- Each email should stand alone — assume they only see one

Specific guidance:
- Email 1: Open with a question about their biggest operations challenge. Mention receivables or stock briefly. One CTA.
- Email 2: Different angle — focus on team access or supplier account management. Reference that Email 1 may have landed at a busy time.
- Email 3: Short. Direct. Offer to close the loop gracefully. "If this isn't the right fit right now, no problem — just let me know."

Output format:
EMAIL 1
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 2
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 3
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]
```

## Finding wholesale business contacts

**Where to find email addresses:**
- LinkedIn (search "distribution manager", "wholesale director", "operations manager" by industry)
- Local business directories and Chamber of Commerce member lists
- Trade association membership directories
- Google Maps: search "[product type] wholesaler [city]", visit their website for contact info
- Industry trade publications often list member companies

**Email verification:**
Before sending, verify emails using a free tool like Hunter.io or NeverBounce.
Sending to invalid emails damages your sender reputation and reduces deliverability.

**Subject line testing:**
For B2B, high-performing subject lines are:
- Specific question: "How do you track receivables across 40+ accounts?"
- Reference a problem: "Distribution businesses lose X hours/week on manual reconciliation"
- First name personalisation: "[Name], quick question about [Company]"

Always test two subject lines by sending half the list to each. Check open rates after 48 hours.

**Following up after a reply:**
Distribution businesses make slower decisions than retail shops. Expect 2–4 weeks from first contact to a decision. Offer a 30-day trial period if you have the infrastructure.
