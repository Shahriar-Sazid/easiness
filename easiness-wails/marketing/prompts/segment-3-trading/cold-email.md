## Prompt

```
You are a marketing copywriter for a software product called Easiness.

I have provided you with context files about the product, its features and benefits, pricing, brand voice, and the target customer.

Write a cold email sequence (3 emails) targeting trading company owners and import/export business managers.

Context for email sequence:
- Email 1 is the first contact — they have never heard of Easiness
- Email 2 is a follow-up if they did not reply to Email 1 (send 5 days later)
- Email 3 is the final follow-up if still no reply (send 5 days after Email 2)

Key characteristics of this audience:
- They deal with overseas suppliers where disputes are costly and hard to resolve
- Their deals are large — one mistake has significant financial impact
- They care about data privacy — their supplier relationships and pricing are sensitive
- They are business-savvy and expect professional communication
- Decision cycles are 4–8 weeks — they move carefully
- They have likely tried multiple software solutions and been disappointed

Requirements for all three emails:
- Keep each email under 200 words — short and precise
- Tone: peer-to-peer, professional, direct. Like a message from a fellow business person.
- Do NOT use: "I hope this email finds you well", "synergy", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "solution"
- Lead with a specific business risk or cost — make them feel the problem before offering the solution
- Reference privacy and data ownership at least once across the three emails
- Do NOT try to close a sale in a cold email — aim for a reply or a call booking
- Each email should be usable standalone

Specific guidance:
- Email 1: Open with the profit clarity problem — after a large deal, do they know the exact margin? Lead with a question. One CTA.
- Email 2: Shift to supplier account protection — how do they prove what they paid if a dispute comes up? Slightly more specific about the product.
- Email 3: Short and respectful. Offer to close the loop. Mention self-hosted option briefly — their data, their server.

Output format:
EMAIL 1
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 2
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]

---

EMAIL 3
Subject: [subject line]
Body:
[email body]
```

## Finding trading company contacts

**Where to find email addresses:**
- Chamber of Commerce member directories (many publish member lists with contact details)
- Import/export association websites
- Trade directories: Kompass, Europages, Alibaba supplier profiles
- LinkedIn: search "[commodity] trader [city]", "[product] importer [country]"
- Government trade databases: in many countries, import/export registrations are public records

**Personalisation for higher reply rates:**
Trading company owners respond much better to personalised emails. Before sending:
- Reference their industry or product type: "I see you import [product category]..."
- Reference a real challenge in their industry: "Fluctuating freight costs have made margin tracking harder this year..."
- Use their company name in the email body, not just the greeting

**Sales cycle expectation:**
Trading companies are slow to decide. Your goal is not to sell in the first email — it is to start a conversation. Be patient, follow up consistently but not aggressively, and offer a demo instead of a purchase.

**Competitive positioning:**
If they mention they use QuickBooks, SAP, or another ERP:
- QuickBooks: doesn't include inventory management; can complement Easiness
- SAP/Oracle: too complex and expensive for their size; Easiness is purpose-built for SME trading
- Excel: no automatic profit tracking, no running balances, no PDF invoices
